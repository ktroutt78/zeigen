## Ideas from Cap (2026-11-15)

Seen in Cap while recording the reference clip. Study Cap's approach (AGPLv3: approach only, don't copy code).

- **Export progress.** Show frames done out of total, a circular progress indicator that fills, and an estimated time remaining while exporting.
- **Cursor smoothing.** Let Keith choose how smooth mouse movement looks (Cap has presets plus custom). Related to the zoom work: the camera reacts to the cursor, so whichever smoothing the cursor gets should be consistent with the camera path.
- **Cursor style.** Let Keith pick the cursor icon used in the export.
- **Scene changes on the timeline.** A Scene track (like Cap's, under the Zoom track) where Keith marks stretches of the video to switch layout, with an animated transition each way. The main case: cut from the screen recording to full-screen webcam (talking to camera), then transition back to the screen recording. Also: hide the webcam bubble and bring it back, and switch between full screen and partial screen.
- **Zoom gets its own timeline row.** Today the zoom capsules float in an unlabelled lane under the waveform: no row label, no track background, and they don't read as part of the timeline. Keith wants a real track like Cap's stacked Video / Zoom / Scene rows: a labelled "Zoom" row with a full-width track background, the same height and styling as the video track, and the playhead running through every row. Same idea as the Scene track, so plan the timeline layout once for both rather than bolting on rows one at a time. (The reason labels themselves fit fine on the segments Keith tested.) Carried over from the zoom-polish plan, not done there: widen the grab area on the zoom segment's resize handles. They are 6px wide today (`SegmentTrack.tsx`, `EdgeHandle`) and hard to hit; do it as part of this row redesign.
- **Clean window drag while recording.** Since window recordings started capturing popups (2026-11-15), dragging the recorded window makes it shake in the frame and crossing displays is rough for about a second (details and measurements in `docs/DEBT.md`). What was learned: the new capture is a display capture cropped to the window, and a crop can only follow a window after it has moved, so polling faster shrinks the wobble (19px to 13px going from 30 to 120 checks a second) but cannot remove it. The old single-window capture follows a drag perfectly, including across displays, because its frame is the window. **Hybrid idea, started and stopped unfinished:** use the new capture while the window is still and switch the running recording to the old single-window capture while it is moving (no popup can be open during a drag anyway), holding the last good frame for the few frames around each switch so no half-switched frame is written. Unknown: whether macOS lets a running recording switch between those two kinds of capture cleanly; switching between displays this way did work. The other route is to record a margin around the window and steady the picture at export from each frame's real position, which touches the export, cursor mapping and corner handling. Low priority: Keith rarely drags a window while recording.
- **Timeline zoom.** Zeigen's timeline always fits the whole recording to the window width (`SegmentTrack.tsx` maps the full duration to the track width), so you can't zoom in. Add zoom-in for precise edits on long recordings, with a one-click "fit whole video" to get back. Cap has a zoom slider next to the timeline. This also helps the "fiddly zoom editing" complaint: on a 6-minute recording a 6px handle covers a lot of time.
- **Auto-zoom on a button.** Cap only adds auto zooms when you press Generate on the Zoom track. Zeigen runs detection automatically when Review opens (`Review.tsx:1304`). Decide together with the auto-zoom reasons work (Problem 2).
